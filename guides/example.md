Read @austin1961a:

-  ["Performative Utterances"](https://canvas.illinoisstate.edu/courses/53417/files/8923380/download?download_frd=1)

This was first published in Austin's *Philosophical Papers*, a collection of his
work published shortly after his unexpected death from lung cancer, at the age
of 48, in 1960. It is a lightly edited transcript of a lecture he gave on the
topic on the BBC in 1956. I have not been able to find the original audio of
that lecture. The Harvard Philosophy Department has posted [the audio from a talk
on the subject that Austin gave at the University of Gothenberg, Sweden, in
1959](https://youtu.be/JXo0YNZ3WsE?si=zGByqrD5IORHVof7), if you are curious.

I also want you to read a short selection from Lecture VIII of @austin1962. It
amounts to about 12 pages---I've crossed out the paragraphs that you are free to
skip. This is from a series of lectures he gave in 1955. In this short
selection, he makes a distinction between *locutionary*, *illocutionary*, and
*perlocutionary* acts. (In the crossed out sections, he further subdivides
locutionary acts into three kinds---it is interesting, and connects back to
Frege!)

-  [Lecture VIII](https://canvas.illinoisstate.edu/courses/53417/files/8923627/download?download_frd=1)
